# Coach algorithm

How PokerWiz decides whether a play was good or bad. The code lives in `src/coach/`. It runs in a web worker (`coach.worker.js`), and its entry point is `analyzeHand(record)`.

The coach is **math first**. Every graded decision comes down to pot odds, equity against ranges and expected value. **Player traits** change the inputs to that math: how wide ranges are, how often players fold, bluff or raise, and how sharp their decisions are. They never override the math.

## Pipeline

```
HandRecorder (coach mode) ──> record ──> analyzeHand
                                           │
      replay.js    replays every action through the betting engine (utils/handEngine.js)
      profiles.js  trait meters -> model parameters (per villain, plus your table image)
      preflop:     preflopModel.js narrows villain ranges; grades hero spots against charts
      postflop:    boardStrength.js scores every combo on the board
                   postflopModel.js narrows villain ranges from their actions
                   decisions.js values every hero option in chips (EV)
      equity.js    Monte Carlo equity vs weighted ranges (seeded, so results repeat)
      grading.js   EV loss -> score -> Best / Good / Inaccuracy / Mistake / Blunder
      explain.js   plain-language notes
```

## 1. Ranges are weights on all 1326 combos

Each villain starts with every combo except the ones holding your cards, at weight 1. Every action they take multiplies each combo's weight by the likelihood that a player of their type takes that action with that combo (Bayes, without the normalizing constant). Nothing ever hits zero, so a surprising line still keeps some unlikely hands in range.

Hand classes (AKs, QQ, T9o...) come from `combos.js`. `preflopTable.js` is generated by `scripts/generate-preflop-table.mjs`. It ranks all 169 starting hands by a blend of three things:

- equity vs a random hand (45%)
- equity vs the top 20% of hands (55%)
- a small playability bonus for suited and connected hands

Every class gets a slice of the 1,326 combos. That turns "top 15%" into a concrete set of hands.

## 2. Preflop

Baseline charts from solver research, as a share of all hands:

| Spot | Numbers |
| --- | --- |
| Open raise, 6-max | UTG 15%, HJ 21%, CO 28%, BTN 45%, SB 38% |
| Open raise, 9-max | UTG 10%, rising to the same late-position values |
| Big blind defense vs button | about 55% total, of which 11.5% is 3-bets |
| Earlier opens | defended progressively tighter |
| Small blind, in-position players | mostly 3-bet or fold |
| Facing a 3-bet | continue with about 45% of your opening range; 4-bet about 2.5% plus 6% of the open width |
| Facing a 4-bet | only the top 3-4% continues |

Player traits bend these numbers:

- `widthMult` scales every threshold. It comes from VPIP: 10% for a nit up to 55% for a maniac, where 24% (a regular) = 1.
- `pfrRatio` splits raises from calls. Passive players limp and flat more, and slow-play some big hands.
- `noise` softens the edge of each range. Amateurs blur around the boundary; pros stay sharp.
- **Opener width:** against a loose opener, both the villains' ranges and the hero's recommended ranges widen (by the square root of the opener's width multiplier).

Hero preflop spots are graded by how far the hand sits from the edge of the right action. Folding a hand that should play is penalized 3x, and folding a premium gets an extra penalty. When stacks go in (facing an all-in, or a call that puts you all in), preflop is graded on pure EV instead: equity against the shover's range versus the price.

## 3. Postflop hand strength (`boardStrength.js`)

For every combo on the board, the coach computes three numbers:

- **`hs`:** the combo's percentile among all possible combos on this board.
- **`draw`:** counted outs, converted with the rule of 4 on the flop and the rule of 2 on the turn. Outs are:
  - cards that make a straight or better: 1 out each
  - cards that make a set or trips with a hole card: 0.7
  - cards that make two pair with a hole card: 0.5
  - overcards that pair: 0.4
- **`eff`:** `hs + (1 - hs) * draw`. This is the number players are modeled to act on.

## 4. Postflop behavior (`postflopModel.js`)

**Facing a bet.** The required equity is `call / (pot after the bet + call)`.

- The player estimates their equity against the range they put the bettor on. That range is a value region plus some bluffs.
- **Value region** starts at `0.97 - 0.42 * exp(-0.9 * size/pot)`: about 0.66 for a third-pot bet, 0.80 for a pot bet, 0.90 at 2x pot. Raises add 0.10, and multiway adds 0.03 per extra player.
- **Bluffs** are assumed at `0.75 * required / foldMult`. Populations under-bluff big bets. Stations (low `foldMult`) believe you bluff more; nits believe you bluff less.
- **Threshold** to continue: `valueStart + (1 - valueStart) * (required - bluffs) / (1 - bluffs)`. If they believe you bluff at least as often as the required equity, anything that beats air calls, but the floor still rises with bet size: `0.25 + 0.3 * size/pot * foldMult + 0.1 * required`. A station calls a third-pot bet with bottom pair, not a 2x-pot overbet.
- MDF (`pot / (pot + bet)`) is kept in the code as the theoretical benchmark.

**Nobody has bet.**

- **Value threshold** follows the same size curve, shifted by aggression.
- **Bluffs:** air bluffs scale with `bluffMult`, and draws semi-bluff.
- **Donk bets** are rare. A player acting before the previous street's aggressor rarely leads into them: bet frequency x0.12 for pros, up to x0.47 for amateurs. That's why checking to the raiser tells you almost nothing.

**Raises.** The raise threshold follows the size curve too, so an all-in check-raise means close to the nuts. Raise edges are sharper than call edges, and calls facing a raise are sharper again.

## 5. Valuing hero options (`decisions.js`)

| Option | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Fold | 0 |
| Check | `eq * R * pot + future / 2` |
| Call | `eq * R * (pot + call) - call + future` |
| Bet / raise to X | `P(all fold) * pot + P(called) * (eqCalled * R * potCalled - chips added + future)` |

- **Bet sizes compared:** 33%, 50%, 75%, 100% and 150% of the pot; raises to about 0.6, 0.85 and 1.1 of a pot-sized raise; plus all in.
- **Fold chances** come from each villain's continuing range at that size (the section 4 thresholds), multiplied together for multiway pots.
- **`eqCalled`** is your equity against only the part of their range that continues.
- **Realization `R`:** in position about 1.0; out of position 0.86 on the flop, 0.92 on the turn and 0.82 preflop. River and all-in are 1.0. Each extra opponent multiplies it by 0.96.
- **Future value `future`:** `(eq - 0.5) * 2 * min(stack behind, pot) * factor`, with factor 0.35 on the flop, 0.2 on the turn and 0 on the river. This covers implied and reverse-implied odds, and stops the model preferring a shove just because it ends the hand now.

## 6. Grading (`grading.js`)

- **EV loss** = best option minus your option.
- **Tolerance** before anything counts: 3% of the pot for amateurs, down to 1% for pros. It comes from your Skill meter.
- **Score:** `100 * exp(-3.5 * loss/pot - 0.025 * loss/bb)`. Being wrong by a big share of the pot counts, and so does losing a lot of big blinds.
- **Labels:** Best at 97+, Good at 80+, Inaccuracy at 55+, Mistake at 30+, Blunder below 30.
- **Hand accuracy** is a weighted average of the decisions, weighted by the square root of the pot in big blinds, so big pots count more.

## Reads: tendency + skill level (`profiles.js`)

A read has two parts, chosen separately: **how they play** (tendency) and **how good they are** (skill level). Under them are five meters, each from 0 to 100:

- Looseness and Aggression (set by the tendency)
- Skill (set by the level)
- Tilt
- Form (cold run ... heater, with 50 as neutral)

Tendencies aim at typical tracker numbers (VPIP / PFR, aggression):

| Tendency | VPIP / PFR | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unknown | 24 / 19 | an average player |
| Nit | 14 / 11 | |
| TAG | 22 / 19 | |
| LAG | 32 / 26 | aggression 3-5 |
| Loose passive | 35 / 10 | |
| Calling station | 40 / 8 | aggression below 1.5 |
| Maniac | 50 / 40 | aggression 5+ |
| Drunk | very loose | extra noise, folds 30% less, bluffs 30% more and at any size |

Skill levels: Beginner (5), Recreational (22), Regular (50), Strong regular (75), Pro (95). Skill sets:

- **Sharpness** (`noise`): pros follow their thresholds closely, beginners are all over the place.
- **Calling:** folding is pulled toward the correct amount as skill rises; recreational players call noticeably more (x0.75 at skill 0).
- **Big bluffs** (`bigBluffShy`): below strong-regular level, bluffing drops off as bets get bigger (`exp(-3 * shy * (size - 0.4))`). A recreational player's pot-size bet is almost never a bluff, and their big bets come from clearly strong hands (sharper edge, slightly higher threshold). Tilt brings big bluffs back.
- **Draws** (`drawAggro`): recreational players check and call their draws, even big combo draws. Bets and raises are judged on made-hand strength (`hs`) instead of strength plus draw equity (`eff`), and semi-bluffs are scaled down. Strong players bet and raise draws.

Mood and image:

- **Tilt** widens ranges (up to +60%), raises aggression (+50%) and bluffs (x2), cuts folding (-30%) and adds noise. This follows the tilt research: more hands, more aggression, ignoring pot odds.
- **Heater** makes a player a bit wider and more aggressive, and less likely to fold.
- **Cold run** makes a player tighter and more passive, and more likely to fold ("scared money").
- **Your table image** (your own Looseness and Aggression, plus visible tilt) changes how often villains fold to you. A wild image gets fewer folds, a nitty image gets more.
- **Your level** sets how strict the grading is.

## Practice mode (`src/practice/generateSpot.js`)

You pick a game (cash: 6-max, $1/$2, 100 bb; tournament: 9-max, 1/2 with a big-blind ante, 30 bb), a spot (preflop, heads-up, 3-way) and a read and stack for each opponent. Preflop uses one read for the whole table.

- **Preflop:** every seat is dealt; players before you act on their real cards, sampling fold / call / raise from the preflop model's likelihoods for their hand. Half the time the bottom 40% of hands is redealt to you, so fewer spots are automatic folds.
- **Heads-up / 3-way:** the first player in preflop order opens and the others call. Each player's cards are dealt by rejection sampling so they fit that action for their read. Then a street is picked (flop 45%, turn 30%, river 25%) and everyone plays on to your turn: villains sample check/bet (random standard size, overbets for wild players) or fold/call/raise from the postflop model with their real cards. Your earlier streets are played by a solid regular; spots where it would fold are redealt.
- **Playing it out:** from the spot on you play the hand to the end. After each of your actions villains answer with the same models (preflop chart likelihoods preflop, the postflop model after). The next cards are dealt at random or picked by you. A picked card that a villain holds is swapped out of their hand for a random card, so the picker never reveals their cards. The hand ends at a fold (yours ends it right away) or at showdown.
- **Grading:** the finished hand goes through `analyzeHand` like any other hand. Only your decisions from the spot on are graded (earlier streets were played for you); the hand's accuracy is the usual pot-weighted average. Villain cards still in the hand are revealed.

## Reviewing saved hands (the Coach button in Hands)

Every No-Limit Hold'em hand in Hands has a Coach button. `savedHand.js` turns the saved hand into a record the coach can replay.

Hands recorded with the current recorder already have seats and stacks. Older hands and the samples only have the action log, so the missing parts are rebuilt from it:

- **Players:** everyone who acted, in order. Anyone who never acted folded before the action.
- **Table size:** 9-max if a 9-max-only seat (UTG+1, MP, LJ) appears, otherwise 6-max.
- **Stacks:** the log is first replayed with bottomless stacks to measure how much each player put in.
  - A player who shoved gets exactly the stack that puts them all in at that point.
  - Everyone else gets 100 bb, or what they put in plus 50 bb (rounded up to 50 bb) when that's more.
- **Reads:** the reads from your last review of that hand (`coachReads`); otherwise villains are average regulars.
- **Checks:** the rebuilt log must replay cleanly through the betting engine, or the hand is declined with a clear message.

The review screen lists every assumption. It has quick read chips for each villain and for your level; changing one re-runs the analysis while the old report stays visible. The score (`coachAccuracy`) and the reads are saved onto the hand. The score shows in the hand list and on the hand, and feeds the "Coach's Pick" highlight.

Omaha hands show a disabled Coach button with the reason, because ranges and hand evaluation here are Hold'em-only.

## Calibration

Run `node scripts/test-coach.mjs`, or add `--verbose` to see every note. Add `--api http://localhost:3001` to also review every hand saved in your database. It plays scripted hands with an obvious right answer and prints each decision's grade, the best line and the EV of every option.

Current results:

| Scripted hand | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Value-betting top pair three streets | 98% |
| Folding AA preflop | 1 (Blunder) |
| Opening 72o under the gun | 0 |
| River bluff with air vs a calling station | Mistake |
| Same river bluff vs a nit | best play |
| Calling a nit's river check-raise all-in with one pair | Blunder |
| Folding the nut flush draw to a small bet | Blunder |
| Calling a maniac's short shove with 22 | correct |

Add a case whenever a verdict looks wrong, then tune.

## Known gaps / next steps

- **Villain re-raises** aren't modeled when valuing hero bets. The calling range already includes the strong hands, but a bluff that gets raised loses more than the model says.
- **Future streets** are a single "future value" term, not a game tree. A proper two-street lookahead (or solver data) would fix thin-value and slow-play spots.
- **Preflop ranking** blends equities with a heuristic playability bonus. Replacing it with real solver ranges per position would sharpen preflop grading.
- **Short stacks** (under 25 bb) should switch to push/fold charts preflop.
- **Blockers** aren't used explicitly. Card removal is only handled through sampling.
- **PLO** isn't supported by the coach; the evaluator and ranges are Hold'em only.
- **Sizing against sticky players:** against calling stations and recreational players the coach prefers big value bets and grades half-pot value bets hard. Real players fold more of their weak hands to big bets than the calling floor says; a better continuing-range model would soften this.
- **Tournament ICM** isn't modeled: tournament spots are graded on chips, and preflop charts don't adjust for the ante.
- **Villain grading** ("how well did they play") would reuse the same machinery.
- **Profile learning:** trait meters could be filled in from a villain's past hands and stored per opponent.
